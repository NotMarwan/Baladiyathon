# agent3 — COGNITIVE, SLEEP & STRESS supplements

## 1. L-Theanine
- What: Non-protein amino acid from tea (Camellia sinensis); alpha-wave promoter, calming without sedation.
- Evidence tier: **Strong** for acute anxiety reduction + attention under stress; **Moderate** for sleep quality.
- Effective dose: **100–200 mg** single dose, or **200–400 mg/day** split. 100 mg is the sweet spot when stacked with caffeine (1:1 or 2:1 ratio).
- #1 caveat: Don't expect a sedative — it modulates alpha waves, not GABA directly. Skip if you need it to "knock you out."
- Source: https://examine.com/supplements/theanine/ (24 trials, 1,610 participants, Evidence Grade A for stress signs/symptoms)

## 2. Melatonin — the dose most sold IS wrong
- What: Endogenous pineal hormone; signals darkness, doesn't sedate. Most OTC pills (3–10 mg) produce supraphysiologic blood levels.
- Evidence tier: **Strong** for shortening sleep onset (~7 min) and jet-lag; **Moderate** for total sleep time (+8 min) and sleep quality in primary insomnia. Meta-analysis n=1,683.
- Effective dose: **0.3–0.5 mg** taken 2–3 h before desired sleep. 0.5 mg is roughly physiological; doses above 1 mg show no extra benefit on sleep onset but increase next-day grogginess. Higher doses (2–5 mg) only justified for circadian-rhythm disorders (DSPS) or blind-free-running rhythms.
- #1 caveat: **Label fraud is rampant** — 2017 Canadian study (Erland & Saxena, JCSM) found 71% of 31 melatonin products were off by >10%, range −83% to +478%; 8 also contained unlabeled serotonin.
- Sources: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3656905/ (Ferracioli-Oda meta-analysis, PLoS One 2013); https://examine.com/supplements/melatonin/

## 3. Magnesium glycinate vs threonate
- What: Two organic forms often marketed for "calm/sleep" (glycinate) or "memory/brain" (threonate, L-threonate salt = "Magtein"). Elemental Mg ≈ 14% in glycinate, 8% in threonate.
- Evidence tier: **Glycinate — Weak** for sleep; the molecule is mostly used because it's gentler on the gut and tolerates 200–400 mg elemental Mg/day with less diarrhea than oxide. **Threonate — Weak**: only 1 small human trial (Slutsky 2010, n=44) showed CSF Mg rise and modest memory gains; no replication at scale.
- Effective dose: **200–400 mg elemental Mg** at night, regardless of form. RDA adults 310–420 mg; ~50% of US adults fall short per NHANES.
- #1 caveat: Threonate is expensive and you'll need ~2 g of the salt to hit 144 mg elemental Mg — most marketed doses are too low to matter. Glycinate's edge over citrate is GI tolerability, not superior anxiolysis.
- Source: https://examine.com/supplements/magnesium/ (Examine, May 2025)

## 4. Ashwagandha (KSM-66 vs Sensoril)
- What: Withania somnifera root extract, adaptogen. Two flagship patents: **KSM-66** (root only, ~5% withanolides, 600 mg/day dose) and **Sensoril** (root+leaf, ~10% withanolides, 250–500 mg/day dose).
- Evidence tier: **Strong** for stress/anxiety/cortisol reduction (29 trials, 8,803 participants, Examine Grade A for cortisol). **Moderate** for VO2max, strength, testosterone in men; **Weak** for sleep latency, cognition.
- Effective dose: **300–600 mg/day** standardized extract (≥5% withanolides), taken with food, 8+ weeks.
- #1 caveat: **Case-report organ rejection in kidney-transplant patient** (Examine, citing transplant literature). Avoid if immunosuppressed, on thyroid meds (may raise T3/T4), pregnant, or with autoimmune disease. Watch for hepatotoxicity case reports. Also: products vary wildly in withanolide content even when "standardized."
- Source: https://examine.com/supplements/ashwagandha/

## 5. Rhodiola rosea
- What: Arctic root adaptogen; SHR-5 extract (standardized to rosavin 3%, salidroside 1%) is the most-studied form.
- Evidence tier: **Moderate** for mental fatigue/burnout; **Weak-Moderate** for exercise endurance and cognitive fatigue. Bell-curve dose-response.
- Effective dose: **200–400 mg/day** standardized extract (SHR-5 or equivalent). Acute fatigue: 288–680 mg. **Do NOT exceed 680 mg** — higher doses become ineffective.
- #1 caveat: Possible additive serotonergic effect with SSRIs/antidepressants (Examine "severe, possible"). Heavy adulteration in supply chain — species substitution and under-dosing are common.
- Source: https://examine.com/supplements/rhodiola-rosea/

## 6. Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus)
- What: Culinary/medicinal mushroom; hericenones + erinacines theorized to stimulate NGF.
- Evidence tier: **Weak**. Only 3 RCTs total (~101 participants). Two small studies (Mori 2009 n=30, Saitsu 2019) suggest mild cognitive benefit in MCI or 50+ adults; effects in healthy young adults are unproven.
- Effective dose: **1,000–3,000 mg/day** of fruiting-body extract, divided 3 doses. Standardized to ≥30% polysaccharides.
- #1 caveat: Most cognitive claims extrapolated from in-vitro NGF work, not human outcomes. Wild-harvested products vary 5× in beta-glucan content. Not unsafe, but evidence is thin.
- Source: https://examine.com/supplements/lions-mane/

## 7. Magnesium (general) — bonus
- Effective dose: 200–400 mg elemental Mg/day; split doses to avoid osmotic diarrhea.
- #1 caveat: Form matters less than total elemental Mg and tolerability. Oxide is ~4% absorbed; glycinate, citrate, malate are well-absorbed.

---

**TL;DR**
- **Worth taking:** Melatonin 0.3–0.5 mg for sleep onset (anything more is wasted); L-theanine 100–200 mg for stress/attention; Ashwagandha KSM-66 600 mg/day for stress/cortisol.
- **Skip:** 5–10 mg melatonin gummies, "Magtein" memory claims, Lion's Mane unless you enjoy placebo + mushrooms.
- **What people get wrong:** More melatonin = more sleep (false — bell curve, more = groggier); magnesium form drives the marketing hype far more than the clinical effect; "standardized" ashwagandha still varies 10× in actual withanolide content; L-theanine is not a sleeping pill.
