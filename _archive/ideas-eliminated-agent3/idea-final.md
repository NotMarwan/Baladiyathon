## 1. THE IDEA

### Name (Arabic + English) and one-line pitch

# **مِرْآة ٱلْمَدِينَة — MIR'AT AL-MADINA**
### *"The City Mirror" — the national 4D digital twin that lets Saudi Arabia see, scrub and orchestrate the future of every street.*

**One-line pitch:**
> A real-time, three-dimensional, time-scrubbable digital twin of the entire city — a "mirror" of every road, every dig, every car, every person — that doesn't just *show* the next 24 hours of Riyadh's traffic, it *conducts* it. Every new excavation permit, every paving job, every utility cut is fed into the mirror before approval. The mirror simulates the future, re-times the dig into a shared trench at a quieter hour, and pushes the optimised plan to citizens, navigators and MOMRAH inspectors in the same second. Riyadh doesn't *react* to its own construction anymore — it *composes* it like an orchestra.

## 2. THE SIGNATURE WOW MOMENT (the single beat that wins the room)

**Setting:** A dark hall. The judges sit in front of a 4-metre-wide LED wall. The room falls silent. A 30-second ambient track plays.

**Beat 1 — 0:00–0:10 — "It is alive."**
The LED wall fades up. Riyadh materialises in real-time, 3D, in front of the judges. Every road, every flyover, every block of Olaya, every interchange on the Dammam highway. Tens of thousands of moving cars (each one a real Riyadh trip, anonymised, from the city transport data feed). Dig sites glow a soft orange, like bioluminescent jellyfish. Permit-pending sites pulse a soft blue. The air-traffic-control tower for the entire city is alive on screen. The judges hear a faint hum — the sound of a living metropolis.

**Beat 2 — 0:10–0:25 — "Draw a future."**
The presenter walks to a touch console. She draws a new road closure with her finger on the live city: "King Fahd Road, both directions, Saturday 14:00–18:00, for water-main repair." The mirror instantly **stops the present**, splits the screen into two, and shows: on the left, the city as it is *right now*; on the right, the city *with her closure* at 14:00 Saturday. The right side turns red. The orange-glow dig sites ring like a siren.

**Beat 3 — 0:25–0:40 — "Scrub time."**
She flicks her finger — the time-scrubber. 14:00 → 15:00 → 16:00 → 17:00 → 18:00 → 19:00. The judges watch congestion *bloom* across the city in real-time 3D. Then 19:00, the closure ends, congestion recedes. The judges have just *seen the future* of the city — without leaving their seats.

**Beat 4 — 0:40–0:55 — "The mirror answers."**
The mirror speaks. A calm, authoritative voice: *"Mirror recommendation: reschedule this water-main repair to next Friday 22:00–04:00, co-locate with a 3-week-old telecom permit on the same corridor. Predicted impact: −41% on King Fahd Road, −27% on parallel arterials, save ~12,400 driver-hours. Citizen impact: 4,200 SMS to be sent, 28 businesses to be notified in advance."* The judge sees the new plan on the map. Orange → blue. Blue → green. The city re-balances.

**Beat 5 — 0:55–1:10 — "The whole orchestra, not just the violin."**
She presses one more button. A panel slides in from the right: *"All current Riyadh permits — 6,200 active — modelled against the next 30 days."* The mirror's whole brain lights up. It displays the **city's full construction orchestra** for the next 30 days — every dig, every paving job, every utility cut — laid out on a single time-space canvas. The judges see the *whole symphony* in one view. They realise: this isn't a road-app. It's the city's *operating system*.

**She turns to the judges. Silence. She says three words:**

> **"Mir'at al-madina. The city, mirrored."**

**That's the moment. The judges have just watched the city of 35 million people be made *legible* and *composable* in 70 seconds. They will not forget it. They will not forget the feeling of seeing the future.**

## 3. THE BIG VISION — National flagship / first-in-region, tied to Vision 2030

Saudi Arabia's ambition in Vision 2030 is not to be a smarter country that *imports* smart-city technology from Singapore, Helsinki, Seoul or Barcelona. It is to be the country that **defines** what a smart city is in a desert climate, in a Muslim-majority society, at the scale of a $1 trillion city-build programme. The Quality of Life Program, the National Transport & Logistics Strategy, the Housing Program, NEOM (scaled back but still live), the Diriyah Gate, the Riyadh Metro (176 km, 6 lines, opened 1 December 2024), the 33.7-million-population census of 2024 ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia)) — every one of these flagship programmes is a *building block*. **None of them is the operating system that ties them together.** That is the role of MIR'AT AL-MADINA.

> *MIR'AT AL-MADINA is the "glass cockpit" of the Saudi city. It is what every Vision 2030 giga-project will sit on top of. It is the first national-scale 4D digital twin of a major Arab capital — and the first to be *constitutionally* wired into the city's permit-issuing pipeline, not bolted on as a dashboard after the fact.*

This is the "national flagship" beat the brief asks for. It is the first time in the region that a **permit-issuing ministry (MOMRAH)** has ever put a digital twin in the *decision loop* of its own workflow. It is also the first time any city has built a 4D (3D + time) twin with the specific optimisation target of "**co-locate every future dig into shared trenches, before approval**" — which is a Saudi-first innovation, not a Dubai / Singapore copy.

## 4. WHY REVOLUTIONARY + WHY NOW — enabling tech + the gap no one filled

The enabling technology stack has all matured inside a 36-month window (2023–2026):

1. **Balady already has the permit layer.** MOMRAH's own platform runs **19 infrastructure-coordination services** today, including "Future Project Coordination" and "Multiple Excavation Coordination" ([balady.gov.sa/ar/all-products/10497](https://balady.gov.sa/ar/all-products/10497)). The data exists; it just isn't being *simulated*.
2. **Real-time city simulation is now free and proven.** Eclipse SUMO, used by Singapore's Virtual Singapore and 100s of city digital twins worldwide, reached release **1.27.1 on 25 June 2026** and is explicitly documented as supporting "digital twin approaches" ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_of_Urban_MObility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_of_Urban_MObility)).
3. **Open geospatial data is mature.** OpenStreetMap provides a free, community-maintained road-and-building base layer for any city on earth ([openstreetmap.org/about](https://www.openstreetmap.org/about)).
4. **The Riyadh Metro opened on 1 December 2024** with **6 lines and 85 stations** ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh_Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh_Metro)) — and is expected to carry **3.6 million passengers a day at full capacity**. The city's traffic is now multimodal, which means the simulation must also be multimodal. The Riyadh Metro opening is the *trigger event* that makes a 4D twin *necessary*, not optional.
5. **Government ↔ navigation-app pipe is proven.** Waze for Cities has been a "free, two-way data-sharing program used by over 450 governments, departments of transportation, and municipalities" since 2014 ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waze)). The push-channel to citizens exists; Saudi Arabia can plug into it.

**The gap that no one has filled:**
- Today's *Smart City dashboards* (Singapore, Helsinki, Dubai, NEOM The Line) are **read-only** — they show the city, they do not *change* it.
- Today's *Permit systems* (Balady, Dubai's Makanati, Singapore's CorpPass) are **paperwork systems** — they issue forms, they do not *simulate* the consequence.
- **No city in the world has put a 4D digital twin in the *decision loop* of a municipal permit-issuing office.** The only known national 3D twin is Virtual Singapore, which is a planning tool ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_Singapore)). Saudi Arabia, with the world's most-digitised permit system *already built* (G6, G7 in the Sources Ledger), is the first country where a 4D twin can be wired into the permit office in months, not years.

**This is the only Saudi city-scale idea that combines the *mature permit layer* (Balady), the *mature simulation layer* (SUMO), the *mature mapping layer* (OSM), the *mature navigation pipe* (Waze for Cities) and the *mature traffic data* (INRIX, [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIX)) — and turns all five into a single decision-making instrument.**

## 7. HOW IT WORKS — enough architecture to be credible (kept light per the brief)

**Five layers. Each is a battle-tested open standard already deployed at scale.**

| Layer | What it does | Real product, open or commercial |
|---|---|---|
| **Permit ingest** | Pulls every Balady "new excavation", "road works", "sidewalk works", "future project" application in real time | Balady Open API (the same APIs that already power the 19 services on [balady.gov.sa/ar/all-products/10497](https://balady.gov.sa/ar/all-products/10497)) |
| **Geometric twin** | Maintains a 3D mesh of the city: every road, every building, every flyover, the 6 Riyadh Metro lines + 85 stations ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh_Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh_Metro)) | OpenStreetMap PBF + Google Open Buildings + Metro GTFS, rendered in CesiumJS / deck.gl |
| **Traffic simulator** | Runs a microscopic, multi-modal, 4D simulation of the city | Eclipse SUMO 1.27.1 ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_of_Urban_MObility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_of_Urban_MObility)) |
| **Baseline traffic feed** | Real-time and historical traffic from probe data | INRIX AI Traffic, 500M+ vehicles ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIX)) |
| **Optimiser + push channel** | Re-times / re-co-locates dig requests to minimise total city-wide delay; pushes to citizens, navigators, inspectors | Waze for Cities ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waze)) + Balady in-app + WhatsApp Business |

**Two cutting-edge features, not in any of the five layers above, that turn this from a tool into a *flagship*:**

- **Time-scrubbing UI.** A single horizontal slider under the 3D city, the user drags to the future. The city re-renders, in 3D, hour by hour, with the dig, the metro, the traffic, all animated. This is the visual hook — the demo's "wow".
- **A "Conductor" mode.** When the operator presses "Optimise Riyadh", the mirror runs the optimiser across **all** current permit applications simultaneously, returns the *globally optimal* dig schedule for the next 30 days, and renders it on the time-slider. The MOMRAH secretary can then accept, modify, or override the recommendation. This is the *human-in-the-loop* design — the AI advises, the human decides. It is also the part that maps to the *MOMRAH's own existing permit workflow* — meaning the AI plugs in, it doesn't replace the ministry.

That's the whole architecture. Seven paragraphs. No magic. Just five mature systems wired into a single decision loop.
