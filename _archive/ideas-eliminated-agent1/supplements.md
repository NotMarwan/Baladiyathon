# Agent 1 — Foundational Vitamins & Minerals

## Vitamin D3 (Cholecalciferol)
- **What it is:** Fat-soluble prohormone from skin UVB exposure; regulates calcium/bone and immune signaling.
- **Evidence tier:** Strong for correcting deficiency and reducing falls; Moderate for reducing acute respiratory infections/URTI in deficient people; Weak for most other claims (testosterone, depression, weight loss).
- **Effective dose:** 800–2,000 IU/day (20–50 mcg) for maintenance; 4,000 IU/day is the adult tolerable upper intake level (UL).
- **#1 caveat:** D3 raises serum 25(OH)D more effectively than D2. Take with a fat-containing meal for absorption.
- **Source:** https://examine.com/supplements/vitamin-d/

## Vitamin B12 (Methylcobalamin vs Cyanocobalamin)
- **What it is:** Water-soluble cobalamin needed for DNA synthesis, myelin, and red blood cell formation.
- **Evidence tier:** Strong for correcting deficiency and reversing megaloblastic anemia; Moderate for depression when baseline levels are low; Weak for cognitive enhancement in non-deficient people.
- **Effective dose:** RDA 2.4 mcg/day; supplements commonly 500–1,000 mcg/day for vegans, older adults, or people on metformin/PPIs.
- **#1 caveat:** Methylcobalamin is heavily marketed as "superior," but evidence shows cyanocobalamin is more stable and both forms effectively raise B12 status. Cyanocobalamin is generally cheaper and well-studied.
- **Source:** https://examine.com/supplements/vitamin-b12/

## Magnesium (Glycinate / Citrate / Oxide)
- **What it is:** Essential mineral; cofactor in 300+ enzymes affecting nerves, muscles, blood pressure, and glucose.
- **Evidence tier:** Strong for preeclampsia prevention and deficiency correction; Moderate for lowering blood pressure and migraine prevention; Weak for anxiety/stress reduction in otherwise healthy people.
- **Effective dose:** 200–400 mg elemental magnesium/day; RDA 310–420 mg from all sources; supplement UL 350 mg/day.
- **#1 caveat:** Magnesium citrate and glycinate have good bioavailability and are gentle on the gut. Magnesium oxide and carbonate are poorly absorbed and strongly laxative — poor choice for raising body magnesium.
- **Source:** https://examine.com/supplements/magnesium/

## Zinc
- **What it is:** Trace mineral for enzymes, immune function, wound healing, and protein synthesis.
- **Evidence tier:** Strong for shortening common cold duration when started within 24 hours; Moderate for acne; Weak for "immune boosting" in well-nourished people.
- **Effective dose:** RDA 8–11 mg/day; lozenges providing 75–90 mg elemental zinc acetate/gluconate per day can reduce cold duration if started early; daily maintenance supplements usually 15–30 mg.
- **#1 caveat:** Chronic intake >40 mg/day causes copper deficiency and can impair immunity. Take away from high-phytate meals and several antibiotics (separate by 2–6 hours).
- **Source:** https://examine.com/supplements/zinc/

## Iron (Ferrous Bisglycinate vs Sulfate)
- **What it is:** Core component of hemoglobin/myoglobin for oxygen transport.
- **Evidence tier:** Strong for treating iron-deficiency anemia; Weak/None for improving energy or athletic performance in non-deficient people.
- **Effective dose:** RDA 8 mg (men) / 18 mg (premenopausal women); therapeutic doses for deficiency typically 40–65 mg elemental iron 1–2 times daily.
- **#1 caveat:** Ferrous bisglycinate (chelated) causes less nausea/constipation than ferrous sulfate and may be better tolerated, though sulfate is cheaper and has the longest track record. Take on an empty stomach with vitamin C; avoid coffee, tea, dairy, calcium, and antacids around the dose.
- **Source:** https://examine.com/supplements/iron/

## Omega-3 EPA/DHA (Fish Oil)
- **What it is:** Long-chain marine polyunsaturated fats; precursors to anti-inflammatory mediators.
- **Evidence tier:** Strong for lowering triglycerides; Moderate for reducing depression symptoms; Weak for preventing cardiovascular events — fish oil does not reliably reduce heart attacks despite lowering TGs.
- **Effective dose:** 250–500 mg combined EPA+DHA/day for general intake; 1–4 g/day for high triglycerides under medical supervision.
- **#1 caveat:** Risk of atrial fibrillation rises above ~1 g/day in people with or at high risk of cardiovascular disease. Product oxidation and underdosing are common quality issues; ethyl ester forms oxidize more readily.
- **Source:** https://examine.com/supplements/fish-oil/

## TL;DR
- **Worth taking:** Vitamin D3 if sun exposure is low or levels are deficient; vitamin B12 if vegan, older, or on metformin/PPIs; iron only when a clinician confirms deficiency.
- **Skip:** Daily high-dose zinc "for immunity"; fish oil if you already eat fatty fish and have normal triglycerides; magnesium oxide as a magnesium repletion strategy.
- **What most people get wrong:** Taking vitamin D without dietary fat, assuming methylcobalamin beats cyanocobalamin, megadosing zinc above 40 mg, and believing fish oil prevents heart attacks.
