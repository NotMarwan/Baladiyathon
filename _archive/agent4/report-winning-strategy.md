- **Pick the challenge that maximises a working demo + a measurable municipal KPI in 48 hours** (see §6 ranking). My read: **Challenge 1 (Compliance Simulation)** has the best win-probability on the public evidence — but if the team is pure ML/CV, switch to **Challenge 2 (AI Pre-Inspector)**.

- **Team of 5 (not 3, not 7)**: product/PM, full-stack, ML/data, designer/UX, domain/subject-matter expert (municipal or compliance).
- **Idea Card deadline: 14 July 2026 (14 days from this writing).** Don't over-engineer it — they shortlist 15-20 July, so polish, not perfection.

2. Level of innovation and creativity
3. Feasibility and implementability
4. Expected impact and sustainability of the solution

6. Quality of the presentation and team performance

> Six criteria. No weights published. Listed in this exact order.

### 2.2 Cross-reference: MOMRAH Innovation Center ("City Spark") eligibility criteria

MOMRAH's own Innovation Center (momah.gov.sa/en/innovationplatform) uses six parallel criteria for accepting internal ideas — these are the same ministry's stated definition of "good" innovation:

- Alignment with Saudi Vision 2030
- Relevance to Ministry Priorities
- Originality and Innovation
- Concept Readiness for Development
- Scalability and Expandability
- Impact and Added Value

> Implication: MOMRAH values **Vision 2030 alignment + ministry fit + scale + impact** in that order. A Idea Card that opens with Vision 2030 language and ties to a named MOMRAH program (Balady, Furas, Etmam, Sakani, Ejar) will read more "MOMRAH-native" than a purely technical pitch.

- Creativity and innovation

> Note: TAIKAI's #2 ("Technical execution") is absorbed into MOMRAH's #5 ("Quality of the prototype"). The structural similarity is strong — both are 6-criterion rubrics with prototype and pitch as named items.

### 3.3 The "why they won" formula (extracted)

Cross-cutting from the verified winners above:

1. **Real problem, real user.** Every winner solved a pain that the *organiser* had just stated. Quorum answered online-abuse evidence. Porta answered parking. Bit by Bit answered city blight reports. Do not invent a problem MOMRAH did not name.
2. **Working demo over slides.** Memphis's "Bit by Bit" and Hedera's "Major Gainz" both shipped a clickable, deployed artifact. A 3-minute demo of a running app beats 10 minutes of architecture diagrams.
3. **One concrete KPI.** All verified winners showed a defensible number: "we saved 40% on time," "we processed 1,250 reports," "we serve 3 regions." Make the number real and citable.
4. **Domain fit.** EcoFit (real estate → ROSHN) and Porta (transport → Urban Development 2026) both came from the host's own problem space. MOMRAH hackathon → solve a MOMRAH-domain problem. Pick the track that matches your team's real knowledge.
5. **Tech stack = fit-for-purpose, not flashy.** Winners rarely won because of exotic tech. They won because the chosen stack was the *simplest* one that could ship and survive a city-scale demo.
6. **Strong narrative arc.** Quorum's "anonymous report → verified evidence" is a 10-second story. Bit by Bit's "drive + photograph + classify" is another. Your pitch needs a 10-second story.
7. **Pitch rehearsed, team-coordinated.** TAIKAI explicitly calls out that even a great build loses to a poor pitch. Designate one speaker; rehearse the 3-min version 5x.

## 4. Pitch & demo playbook

### 4.1 The 3-minute pitch structure (3 × 60 s, no overruns)

> Timing assumes a 3-minute pitch + 2 minutes Q&A. If you get 5 min, stretch problem and KPI; if you get 90 s, cut KPI.

| Time | Block | Goal | What you say (template) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Hook** | Stop the room. | "Municipal licensing errors in Saudi cost X businesses Y SAR per year. [Demo visual]." |
| 0:15–0:45 | **Problem (MOMRAH-anchored)** | Prove you read the brief. | Quote the official Challenge #N text. Add one statistic from a public Saudi source. |
| 0:45–2:00 | **Live demo** | Show, don't tell. | Click through the running app. Trigger the "wow" moment. No slides. |
| 2:00–2:30 | **KPI + impact** | Make the number real. | "We reduced [metric] from A to B in our test, which at MOMRAH scale = C SAR / year." |
| 2:30–2:50 | **Vision 2030 + MOMRAH fit** | Show you're not just clever. | "Aligned with [Quality of Life Program / Vision 2030 goal X]. Integrates with [Balady/Furas/Etmam]." |
| 2:50–3:00 | **Close** | Land it. | "We are [Team]. We are asking MOMRAH to [one verb]." |

### 4.2 The "wow" moment — what wins it

A 3-min pitch is won or lost in the demo. In the verified past winners:
- **Quorum**: live demo of an anonymous report becoming court-admissible evidence.
- **Bit by Bit**: a real photo of a Memphis pothole uploaded → auto-classified → added to the city's queue.
- **Major Gainz**: a wallet dashboard that scored a DeFi position in real time.

The "wow" is **not** the tech — it is the **user's moment of value**: the citizen's first action that turns into the city's first benefit. Build your demo around that.

### 4.3 Demo do's and don'ts

**Do**
- Demo a **deployed, real URL** (Vercel/Render/Streamlit) — local-host only is a losing bet.
- Have a **backup video** of the demo in case WiFi or auth fails.
- Pre-load all data so the demo is one click deep.
- Show a **before/after** comparison.
- End on the KPI, not on a "thank you" slide.

**Don't**
- Don't open the demo with "as you can see, this is just a prototype" — you told the judges it was a prototype in the brief.
- Don't demo mobile on a phone with a tiny screen. Use a tablet or screen-mirror.
- Don't switch windows. Don't Alt-Tab. Have everything in one tab.
- Don't read slides. Slides are a backdrop, not a script.
- Don't promise ML accuracy you can't defend — judges will ask.

### 4.4 Metrics to show (pick ONE primary, ONE secondary)

Verified past winners led with one of these:
- **Time saved** — "X hours per license / inspection / complaint"
- **% error reduction** — "from A% to B% in our test"
- **SAR saved** — "≈ C million SAR / year at national scale"
- **Coverage** — "covers N municipalities / N types of permits"
- **Adoption potential** — "X existing MOMRAH officers could use this on day 1"

Choose **one**; back it up with a **second** that you only mention if asked.

### 4.5 Common losing mistakes (compiled from TAIKAI + verified losers)

1. **Pitching a feature, not a problem.** Judges forget features; they remember pain.
2. **No deployed demo.** A local Flask server is a coin-flip in the room.
3. **Vague KPI.** "Improves efficiency" is not a number. "Reduces errors by 38% on a 1,000-record test set" is.
4. **Ignoring MOMRAH's own language.** No "Quality of Life" / "Vision 2030" / "Balady" / "Furas" mention = instant low-relevance score.
5. **Over-stuffing the deck.** If you have 12 slides for 3 minutes, the room is reading, not listening.
6. **Solo presenter.** If only one person presents, the team looks fragile. Rotate speakers; rehearse the handoffs.
7. **No data sources cited.** "We trained on a public Saudi dataset" beats "we trained on data."
8. **No edge cases.** "What if a permit is ambiguous?" — if you don't answer it, the next team will.

## 5. Idea-Card optimisation

### 5.1 Form fields (from https://momah.gov.sa/ar/form/hackathon-2026)

| Field | Max chars / notes | Optimisation rule |

| اسم الفكرة (Idea name) | short | ≤ 6 words, must contain the challenge's keyword (e.g., "compliance", "inspection", "traffic") |

### 5.2 Description structure (≈ 600–900 words recommended; read what fits)

Use this template, in Arabic or English. Arabic is recommended because the brief is in Arabic and the judging panel reads Arabic; if your team is not fluent, submit in both or in the language the team can defend in Q&A. The Arabic on the MOMRAH site is Modern Standard — clear, not dialectal.

**1. العنوان (1 line).** Problem + outcome.
> "مراقب رقمي استباقي للمخالفات البلدية باستخدام الرؤية الحاسوبية"

**2. المشكلة (3–4 sentences).** Quote MOMRAH's own challenge text verbatim (one sentence). Add a Saudi-specific public data point (e.g., a Balady stat you can cite from momah.gov.sa).

**3. الحل (4–6 sentences).** What the prototype does, in 1–2 minutes of use, in plain Arabic.

**4. التقنية (2–3 sentences).** Which allowed tech, and why it is the **simplest** that works (LLM-Vision for inspection; RAG over MOMRAH regulations for compliance; CV+optimization for traffic).

**5. الأثر (3–4 sentences, the KPI paragraph).** One number, one source, one scale-up. "في اختبارنا على 1,000 طلب من بيانات [مصدر مفتوح]، انخفضت أخطاء الترخيص من 22% إلى 4%؛ عند تعميمها على [N] بلدية = [M] مليون ريال سنويًا."

**6. الربط برؤية 2030 وبرنامج جودة الحياة (2 sentences).** Name the Vision 2030 program and MOMRAH platform verbatim.

**7. الجدوى (2–3 sentences).** Why this can ship in 48 h and integrate with [Balady / Furas / Etmam] in 90 days.

**8. الفريق (2 sentences).** Roles, not names. "مهندس تعلم آلي، مطوّر Full-Stack، مصمّم، خبير بلدي."

## 6. Team composition for a 2-day build

### 6.1 The recommended 5-person squad

| **Product / PM (lead)** | Municipal-tech or gov-tech experience. Arabic-fluent. | Idea-Card final draft. Pitch script. KPI definition. | Demo flow. Q&A prep. Final polish. |

| **Domain SME** | Municipal licensing, inspection, or traffic — *not* a developer. | Reality-check on problem + KPI. | Defends Q&A. Co-presents. |

> **Why 5?** Al-Baha (momah.gov.sa→baha.gov.sa hackathon page) explicitly required 3–5 members. MOMRAH Baladiyathon 2026 does not state a team size on the form. ROSHN 2023 (Arab News) had 16 teams. Five is the sweet spot: enough parallelism, small enough to coordinate in 48 h.

### 6.2 What to do in the **first 6 hours** (pre-Day-1)

Before the 27 July kickoff (registration closes 14 July, shortlist 20 July):

1. **Lock the challenge.** Use §7 ranking.
2. **Lock the KPI.** One number, defensible.
3. **Lock the data source.** Public Saudi data — momah.gov.sa Open Data (https://momah.gov.sa/ar/open-data) is the first place to look. Do not promise ML on data you don't have.
4. **Build the demo skeleton.** Landing page + one flow. Deploy on Vercel/Render. The skeleton survives even if everything else breaks.
5. **Write the Idea Card.** Don't wait. The form is short; submit by 13 July so you can amend on 14 July if needed.

### 6.3 What to do in the **last 6 hours** of Day 2

1. Record the backup demo video (60 s, no audio cuts).
2. Rehearse the 3-min pitch 3 times.
3. Rehearse Q&A with a hostile audience member.
4. Test the deployed URL on the hotel Wi-Fi.
5. Strip the slides to < 8.

| MOMRAH-internal demand signal | **High** — direct fit with the "Balady" platform (balady.gov.sa) | High — "inspection" is in MOMRAH's title and budget | Medium — also high, but more competed |

| "Real municipal KPI" plausibility | **High** — error-rate on a public regulation set is easy to measure | High — precision/recall on a labelled set | Medium — needs real traffic data; risk of "fake demo" perception |

### 7.2 Ranking by win-probability

> Win-probability = impact × feasibility × differentiation, given the official rubric (§2.5).

| Rank | Challenge | Score (subjective, 0–10) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **C1 — Compliance Simulation** | **8.0** | The "regulations + RAG" approach is well-trodden (so low differentiation), but the public-data availability, the MOMRAH-platform fit, the 48-h MVP-ability, and the testable KPI make it the safest path. The "wow" is *type your business, get the verdict in 5 seconds*. The pitch ties directly to Balady. |
| **2** | **C2 — AI Pre-Inspector** | **7.5** | Higher differentiation (fewer KSA references in the public record) and equally good MOMRAH fit. Loses only because: (a) image/violation data is harder to source publicly; (b) judges may want a clear "before vs after" comparison on real data. If your team has CV strength and a public labelled set, this is the *better* choice. |
| **3** | **C3 — Infra-Traffic Impact** | **6.5** | The Urban Development 2026 hackathon in Al-Khobar (alyaum.com/articles/6645530) already produced traffic winners (Porta, Eco Move, Massar). The same space is crowded. Winning here means clearly beating those teams on a problem the judges have already seen. Choose only if your team has a city-data or optimisation edge. |

> **My recommendation: pick C1 (Compliance Simulation) if in doubt. Pick C2 (AI Pre-Inspector) if you have CV / vision data. Avoid C3 unless you have city-data access.**

### 7.3 Why not the other two?

- **C2 is the dark horse.** It has the highest *differentiation* (less-crowded) and the highest "wow" if you can show live image → risk score. But the data sourcing is the chokepoint. If you have a public Saudi street-imagery dataset, the AI-Pre-Inspector track is arguably the best bet.
- **C3 is the familiar space.** The 2026 Urban Development hackathon (Eastern Province) explicitly named **parking** and **transport** as two of its three tracks. Going into MOMRAH's hackathon with another traffic/parking story will be compared line-by-line. The bar is high.
